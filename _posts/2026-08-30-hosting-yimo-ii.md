What better way to end off the summer than hosting [YIMO](https://yimo-official.org/) II on August 29th and 30th?

YIMO (pronounced *yee-mo*), the Youth International Math Olympiad, is a free math olympiad built by students, for students. Our motivation was simple. Outside the national olympiad pipeline, most countries have nothing like the circuit we are fortunate to have in the US, with contests like PUMaC and HMMT. I cofounded YIMO with [Ryan](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryan-ahn-81322736a/) and [Jun](https://www.linkedin.com/in/hyunjun-yi-3424573a0/) in March 2026, with just an idea and the belief that a competition like this could reach kids in every country and bring equitable access. Now, less than six months later, across our two events we have had over **3,000 participants** from around the world.

Running the contest is mostly unglamorous work, and none of it happens without the staff who show up to proctor, write and test problems, grade, answer questions at odd hours, and keep everything on schedule across every time zone at once. But we love it, because we get to watch the mission grow. A huge shoutout to all our passionate staff who have made this possible. Two events down, and we are only just getting started.

> Mathematical talent is distributed equally among different groups, irrespective of geographic, demographic, and economic boundaries.
>
> Federico Ardila-Mantilla, [*Todos Cuentan: Cultivating Diversity in Combinatorics*](https://www.ams.org/publications/journals/notices/201610/rnoti-p1164.pdf), Notices of the AMS **63** (2016), no. 10

This quote resonates with YIMO's mission because talent is already everywhere, and access is the part we are trying to even out.
